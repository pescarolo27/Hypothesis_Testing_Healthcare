Hypothesis Testing in Healthcare: Drug Safety¶

This project was done in October, 2025.¶

📖 Background¶

Pharmaceutical drugs have become an essential part of our health. Therefore, they need to be safe with little or no adverse effects.

A pharmaceutical company, GlobalXYZ, has just completed a randomized controlled drug trial. To promote transparency and reproducibility of the drug's outcome, they (GlobalXYZ) have presented the dataset to your organization, a non-profit that focuses primarily on drug safety.

💾 The Data¶

The dataset provided contained five adverse effects, demographic data, vital signs, etc. Your organization is primarily interested in the drug's adverse reactions. It wants to know if the adverse reactions, if any, are of significant proportions. It has asked you to explore and answer some questions from the data.

The dataset drug_safety.csv was obtained from Hbiostat courtesy of the Vanderbilt University Department of Biostatistics. It contained five adverse effects: headache, abdominal pain, dyspepsia, upper respiratory infection, chronic obstructive airway disease (COAD), demographic data, vital signs, lab measures, etc. The ratio of drug observations to placebo observations is 2 to 1.

For this project, the dataset has been modified to reflect the presence and absence of adverse effects (adverse_effects) and the number of adverse effects in a single individual (num_effects).

The columns in the modified dataset are:

Column Description
sex The gender of the individual
age The age of the individual
trx The treatment (Drug) and control (Placebo) groups
week The week of the drug testing
wbc The count of white blood cells
rbc The count of red blood cells
adverse_effects The presence of at least a single adverse effect
num_effects The number of adverse effects experienced by a single individual

The original dataset can be found here.

Objectives¶

You work with a non-profit that advocates for pharmaceutical drug safety. One of its tasks is to create reports on drugs independent of the drug manufacturer.
Your organization has asked you to explore and answer some questions from the data collected. You'll conduct several hypothesis tests using Python to determine if the adverse reactions of a hypothetical drug are significant. You'll also check if factors such as age significantly influence the adverse reactions.

Perform hypothesis tests to determine if the adverse effects of a pharmaceutical drug are significant!

1) Determine if the proportion of adverse effects differs significantly between the Drug and Placebo groups, saving the p-value as a variable called two_sample_p_value. 2) Find out if the number of adverse effects is independent of the treatment and control groups, saving as a variable called num_effects_p_value containing a p-value. 3) Examine if there is a significant difference between the ages of the Drug and Placebo groups, storing the p-value of your test in a variable called age_group_effects_p_value.

Summary of Findings & Recommendations¶

This project utilizes statistical techniques, primarily hypothesis tests, to evaluate some key objectives regarding individuals' health data & how it was impacted, if at all, by a new hypothetical drug that has been produced by GlobalXYZ. The company, in the interest of transparency, are seeking an evaluation of the validity & reproducibility of a drug trial they have recently completed. Moreover, they need to know if the drug is safe to use.
Analyses were conducted using data provided to answer such inquiries. More specifically, control & treatment groups were analyzed in relation to whether individuals reported any adverse effects as well as how many they experienced. Other variables, such as peoples' ages, were also incorporated into the analyses.

In this project, there were three main sections for each of the primary objectives. Before analyses were conducted, however, the data was imported & explored at a basic level to gain an initial understanding & identify any inconsistencies that may require preprocessing.

  • For clarity, the Drug & treatment groups refer to the same group of individuals, as do the Placebo & control groups.

Preprocessing¶

Of the 16,103 individuals included in the experiment, nearly 7,000 of them did not have their white-blood-cell & red-blood-cell counts properly recorded. As such, a quick analysis of the distributions of these variables was conducted to determine what imputation method to utilize. For the white-blood-cell counts, there was enough of a skew such that the median was used, whereas the mean was used for the red-blood-cell counts because there was near-perfect symmetry in the distribution.

Analysis I - Adverse Effects Between Drug/Placebo Groups¶

To evaluate how the new drug affected individuals across the treatment & control groups, proportions of individuals in each were analyzed according to whether they experienced adverse effects or not. Firstly, some standard analyses were performed including calculations & visuals. Across these two variables, the proportions were remarkably similar. Note that of the 16,103 individuals, 10,727 of them were in the treatment (drug) group & 5,376 were in the control (placebo) group, which is about a 2:1 ratio.

  • The percentage of individuals (within each control/treatment group) who did not experience adverse effects was about 90.45% & 90.48% in the treatment & control groups respectively.

These figures indicate that there is not a significant difference in these proportions of individuals. Nonetheless, hypothesis testing was utilized to either reinforce or oppose this conclusion.

Given that this section evaluated proportions across two groups, a two-sample proportion z-test was used. In this case, the null hypothesis proposed that the proportion of people who experienced adverse effects was equivalent between the treatment & control groups. The alternative hypothesis suggested that the proportions were different.

  • The hypothesis test obtained a p-value of 0.96. Typically, significance levels are set no higher than 10%. As such, this test indicates that there is no significant evidence from which the null hypothesis can be rejected. Therefore, there is not a significant difference in the proportion of individuals who experienced adverse effects from the drug between the drug & placebo groups.

Analysis II - Number of Adverse Effects Between Drug/Placebo Groups¶

To determine if the drug influenced the number of adverse effects that individuals experienced, the number of effects was analyzed across the treatment & control groups. Once more, some straightforward analyses were performed, the results of which came to similar conclusions as in Analysis I. That is, the proportions of individuals across each number of adverse effects were very similar. For example, the percentage of people (within each treatment/control group) who experienced one adverse effect was approximately 8.91% & 9.04% for the treatment & control groups respectively.

To further substantiate these findings, a chi-square hypothesis test was used. It was used because the goal was to determine if the number of adverse effects is independent of the treatment and control groups. Chi-square tests are often used to assess whether two categorical variables are independent or not.
In this case, the null hypothesis proposed that the number of adverse effects experienced is independent of the group that an individual was in. Conversely, the alternative hypothesis suggested that these variables are not independent.

  • The smallest p-value obtained from the hypothesis test was about 0.50, which is much greater than a typical significance level of five or ten percent. As such, this test indicates that there is no significant evidence from which the null hypothesis can be rejected. Therefore, the number of adverse effects experienced from using the drug is independent of whether an individual is in the control or treatment group.

Analysis III - Ages Across Drug/Placebo Groups¶

Finally, the ages of the involved individuals were analyzed across the treatment/control groups to assess whether the drug impacted people differently according to their age. A brief analysis of ages across these two groups revealed quite similar averages & distributions. The average ages were about 64 years in each group.

Given the nature of the data, a Mann-Whitney U test was used to further quantify this inquiry. The null hypothesis stated that there is not a significant difference between the ages of the treatment & control groups. Naturally, the alternative hypothesis stated there is a significant difference.

  • The resulting p-value was about 0.26 which is noticeably larger than the pre-established significance level of ten percent. As such, this test failed to find substantial evidence with which to reject the null hypothesis. In other words, there is no significant difference between the ages of individuals in the treatment & control groups.

Recommendations¶

These analyses reveal meaningful conclusions regarding some of the effects of the new drug produced by GlobalXYZ, specifically in regard to people experiencing adverse effects, the number of such effects, & people's ages. By evaluating these variables across control & treatment groups, it can be indicative as to the true effects of this drug.
In all three sections of this project, it was determined that there is no significant relationship these three variables & the drug. 1) The proportion of individuals who experienced adverse effects from using the drug was not significantly different than that of those who did not use the drug. 2) The number of adverse effects experienced by individuals who used the drug was found to not be significantly different than the number of effects experienced by people who did not use the drug. 3) The ages of people across the control & treatment groups was not significantly different, therefore, generally ruling out age as a risk factor when using this drug.

As a result, these findings suggest that using the new drug does not pose any immediate concerns in terms of experiencing adverse effects. Furthermore, age is not a major concern, thus implying that the drug can be used by people of various ages. Additional analyses could be done to evaluate exactly how safe this drug is generally & for particular individuals according to different health qualities, demographics, & other specific details.

  • Obviously, the nature of the drug should still be taken into account when it comes to who may be directed/allowed to take this drug. For instance, giving a new drug to young children is likely not a wise decision given that their bodies are still maturing.

The figure above illustrates the percentage of individuals who have experienced adverse effects from the drugs they took in the experimental trial based on what treatment group they were in.

Recall that in this trial, there were about two people in the "Drug" group for every one person in the "Placebo" group. The proportion of people who experienced adverse effects was about 9.55% & 9.52% for those in the drug & placebo group respectively.
As such, this figure suggests that there is no significant difference in the proportion of people who experienced adverse effects between the drug & placebo groups. Nevertheless, hypothesis testing can be utilized to evaluate this further.

Given the goal of this section, it would be appropriate to use a two-sample proportion z-test which tests for equality between proportions across two groups.

  • Such a test requires the groups to be independent. Given that the pharmaceutical company performed a randomized, controlled trial where the patients were only part of either the "Drug" or "Placebo" group, it can be said that the two groups are independent.
  • Each group needs to have a reasonably large sample size. The "Drug" & "Placebo" groups recorded data for about 10,700 & 5,400 individuals respectively which are sufficient.

In this case, the null & alternative hypotheses follow as:

  • $H_0$: The proportion of people who experienced adverse effects was equivalent between the Drug & Placebo groups.
  • $H_A$: The proportion of people who experienced adverse effects was different between the Drug & Placebo groups.

Here, the proportions_ztest function from the statsmodels package is useful. It has the following arguments.

  • The number of relevant observations in each group; the count parameter. In this case, this quantifies the number of patients who experienced adverse effects in the Drug & Placebo groups.
  • The number of total observations in each group; the nobs parameter. In this case, this quantifies the number of total patients in the Drug & Placebo groups.
  • Use a 'two-sided' hypothesis test because the desire is to determine if there is a difference in general between the two groups, & not a difference in a specific direction.

This section is seeking to determine whether the group that a patient is in (the 'trx' variable) has an effect on the number of adverse effects they experience from the drug usage. In other words, are the 'trx' & 'num_effects' variables related. Such an inquiry can be measured with a chi-square test which is often used to evaluate whether two categorical variables are independent or not.

In this case, the null & alternative hypotheses follow as:

  • $H_0$: The number of adverse effects experienced is independent of the group that the patient was in.
  • $H_A$: The number of adverse effects experienced is not independent of the group that the patient was in.

Here, the chi2_independence function from the pingouin package is useful. It has the following arguments.

  • The data parameter takes the relevant dataset.
  • The x & y parameters take the target & explanatory variables respectively.
  • The correction parameter specifies whether or not to apply Yates' continuity correction, which is a fudge factor for when the sample size is very small & the degrees of freedom is 1. Note that degrees of freedom = (#-response-categories - 1) * (#-explanatory-categories).
    • Since each of the two groups has over 5,000 data points, applying this correction is unnecessary.
  • The function returns 3 data frames: the expected counts, the observed counts, & statistics related to the test.

When performing hypothesis testing with numerical variables, evaluating their distributions can help to determine what kind of test is most appropriate. For example, parametric tests rely on the data being normally distributed, whereas non-parametric tests allow for exceptions to this.
In this case, both of these two age distributions are close to, but not quite normal. There is a clear single peak near the midpoint of the age range, but neither of the distributions are symmetrical around the mean in the way that a true normal distribution would appear. Since the mean age is less than the mode of the age (the peak), the data are slightly left-skewed.
As such, a non-parametric hypothesis test would be more appropriate with these variables.

  • To evaluate the normality of these distributions further, other tests & visualizations could be used like a Q-Q plot.

Other factors to consider when choosing a hypothesis test include whether the data are independent or not, if there are clear outliers, or if the sample size is small. In this case, there are no signifcant outliers. Moreover, the two groups, which account for more than 5,000 observations each, are independent because every patient was only in one group & not both.
Given this information, a Mann-Whitney U test could be appropriate for this data.

The mwu function from the pingouin package can be used to perform such a test. It has the following arguments.

  • The x & y parameters take the numerical data from the two groups respectively.
  • The alternative parameter specifies what kind of hypothesis test is being performed; one-tailed vs two-tailed. In this case, a two-tailed test is applicable because the objective pertains to whether there is a significant difference between the ages of the two groups.

Once more, take a significance value of 0.10. The null & alternative hypotheses are as follows.

  • $H_0$: There is not a significant difference between the ages of the Drug & Placebo groups.
  • $H_A$: There is a significant difference between the ages of the Drug & Placebo groups.
